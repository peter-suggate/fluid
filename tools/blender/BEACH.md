# Beach · afternoon surf

Open **Explore → Water landscapes → Beach · afternoon surf**, or `?scene=beach`.
The editable source is `assets/beach/beach.blend`. Build it before baking the
runtime assets. Sand, three feather palms, dune grass, four coastal rocks,
driftwood and seaweed are Blender geometry. The authoring script exports the
combined sand and rock heightfield to `lib/core/imported-assets/beach-terrain.json`.
The fluid collider and visible shore use the same height functions, including
the exposed and submerged rocks; dry rock geometry samples them more finely.

Four floating seaweed clumps are dynamic rigid bodies with lower bulk density
than seawater. Their wet blades and gas bladders are exported from Blender as
analytic ellipsoid parts. Primary, reflection and shadow rays use the same
skin and resident body transform. The physics uses a compact box envelope;
the proxy is three fluid cells thick (375 mm), has a bulk density of 950 kg/m³
and starts at its hydrostatic draft, clear of the bed. Blender places the thin
visible blades at the proxy's waterline. Individual leaves do not deform.
This is buoyant rigid seaweed, not a flexible
kelp simulation. A capture records the actual GPU poses so Cycles can reproduce
the floating clumps without assigning trajectories or hand-placing them.
The sky uses the renderer's shared cloud layer, with blue daylight and warm sun.
Dry scenery uses SVO refinement depth **2**. Palm source volumes use 12.5 mm
samples, dune grass uses 25 mm and rocks use 40 mm; the fluid cell size remains 125 mm.
Each palm has 27 fronds with curved paired leaflets, a tapered ring-scarred trunk,
leaf bases and fruit. The three placements vary their rotation and ground level.
Rock colour, roughness and normals reuse the repository's photographed CC0
[Poly Haven mineral maps](../../assets/alpine-lake/textures/README.md), packed
into the Blender source and GLB. Their closed bedding forms and the collider
come from the same height function, at independent dry/fluid sample spacings.

The scene selects `uniform-narrow-band-flip`, with rigid coupling enabled,
12.5 cm fluid cells and a 1/60 s step. Initially still seawater is displaced by
a full-width offshore piston, with 0.55 m displacement amplitude and a 3.2 s
period. Its 0.8 s startup and one-period shutdown envelopes soften the
finite wave train. The paddle is hidden in shaded views but remains a moving
solid in the solve. Gravity, pressure projection, particle/grid transfer and
the sloping bed determine the resulting waves and run-up. There is no ocean
modifier, animated heightfield, water inflow or replacement water mesh.

This is a finite coastal patch, with closed side boundaries and no active
wave absorption. Returning waves can interfere later in the 16 s take. The
stroke and period are authored inputs, not a claim of validated offshore wave
height or a calibrated ocean spectrum. Piston wavemaking and startup
transients are described in [USACE's Waves Generated by a Piston-Type
Wavemaker](https://erdc-library.erdc.dren.mil/bitstream/11681/22891/1/CERC%20Reprint%204-71.pdf).
The live renderer and its resolution do not establish complete photorealism;
Cycles previews must be assessed separately from production captures.

```sh
BLENDER='/Applications/Blender 5.2 LTS.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender'
"$BLENDER" -b --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/blender/beach.py
"$BLENDER" -b --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/blender/bake_mesh_volume.py -- --collection beach
npm run generate:assets
node --import tsx tools/blender/export-nbflip-water.ts --scene=beach --time=8 --out=artifacts/beach/water.obj
"$BLENDER" -b --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/blender/beach.py -- --water-mesh artifacts/beach/water.obj --render artifacts/beach/cycles.png
node --import tsx tools/capture-forest-waterfall.ts --scene=beach --time=8 --width=1200 --height=800 --out=artifacts/beach/live.png
node --import tsx tools/probe-beach-motion.ts --time=16 --verify=true
```

The water exporter advances the production solver with the authored rigid
body roster, including its prescribed paddle track. The render script checks
the solver provenance and SHA-256 of the exported water before importing it.
It saves a separate preview `.blend`; the reusable source stays water-free.
The motion diagnostic checks the resident GPU paddle track, seaweed heights,
an offshore crest within two seconds and wave arrival at the visible shoreline.
