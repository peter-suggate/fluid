# Four-brick steady Euler vortex

`npm run probe:dawn:adaptive-volume:vortex` runs a full 16×16×8 all-fine
closed free-slip tank, with dx=0.05 m, square side L=0.8 m, depth 0.4 m,
and peak speed U=6 m/s. Gravity, viscosity and surface tension are zero.
Topology is frozen. This removes adaptive interfaces and geometric surface
transport from the dissipation measurement.

With vessel-local x,y and k=π/L, the exact stationary Euler solution is

```
u = U sin(kx) cos(ky)
v = -U cos(kx) sin(ky)
w = 0
p/rho = U²/4 [cos(2kx) + cos(2ky)] + constant.
```

Its divergence is zero and its advective acceleration equals minus the pressure
gradient. Every wall has zero normal velocity and zero normal derivative of
tangential velocity. The top must be physically closed: an atmospheric free
surface would not satisfy this pressure field. The probe authors the voxel
wall shell, seeds staggered face samples, and derives cell velocities from
their incident faces. Pressure starts at zero and is generated by projection.

The discrete initial face energy is independently checked against the continuum
value rho U² L² depth/4. At the scene's density of 998.2 kg/m³ this is 2299.8528 J.
Energy and field error use face dual-volume quadrature, not collocated-cell
energy. The exact steady field is the reference at every observation time.
The probe reports measured errors without inventing a finite-step acceptance
threshold for this first-order split method.

## Stage measurements over the same 1/60 s physical interval

| Outer dt | Steps | Advection energy loss | Projection energy loss | Total energy loss | Final relative field L2 error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1/60 s | 1 | 1.2544% | 7.2038% | 8.4582% | 4.5049% |
| 1/120 s | 2 | 2.1568% | 3.5767% | 5.7334% | 3.0230% |
| 1/240 s | 4 | 4.3607% | 1.7642% | 6.1249% | 3.1379% |

Each stage loss is summed over all steps and normalized by the same initial
energy. Body forces change no energy. All runs complete with bounded volume,
finite dynamics and unchanged production source fingerprints. Receipt files:
`artifacts/analytic-motion/vortex-dt60.json`, `vortex-dt120.json`,
`vortex-dt240.json`.

At 1/60 s, pressure converges to true relative residual 9.53e−7 and maximum
post-projection divergence is about 3.8e−5 s⁻¹. Projection leaves the analytic
vortex-mode amplitude unchanged while removing the gradient component generated
by velocity advection. This supports a splitting-error explanation, rather
than attributing that removal to a failed pressure solve. The leading continuum
splitting estimate is 0.5(U k dt)² = 7.71% for this single step; its agreement
is approximate because interpolation, tracing, discrete operators and boundaries
also contribute.

The projection contribution halves with dt over the fixed interval, while the
advection contribution grows. Therefore merely taking smaller outer timesteps
does not monotonically remove the damping on this fixed grid. Linear
semi-Lagrangian interpolation depends on fractional cell displacement; more
interpolations can increase cumulative smoothing. The two mechanisms must be
addressed separately. These results establish damping with no moving free
surface, so frozen pressure membership during geometric volume microsteps is
not a complete explanation of the observed flow problem.

A promising temporal improvement is a pressure-aware characteristic predictor:
pressure must influence the transported field before sampling. Subtracting an
old pressure gradient only after advection cannot change the projected velocity,
because the projection removes gradients anyway. Such a predictor needs a
derived pressure update, consistent units and a pressure bootstrap. It is not
implemented here. Neither the reconstruction change nor a spatial interpolation
change should be inferred to fix dam wetting without rerunning the independent
dam benchmark.

The near-zero-step control (`dt=duration=1e−7 s`) retains 99.9999596% of
initial energy and has post-projection relative field error 2.10e−7. This
supports interpolation error that tends to zero per step, rather than another
fixed smoothing filter. It does not eliminate accumulated interpolation loss:
at a fixed grid, its loss per unit time need not vanish with dt. Receipt:
`artifacts/analytic-motion/vortex-zero-dt.json`.

## Bounded cubic velocity sampling

The production face predictor now attempts a tensor cubic sample on certified
complete, open, uniform interior MAC stencils with liquid support. Unsupported
stencils retain the established linear sampler. The cubic observation is
clamped component by component to the range of the eight bracketing MAC nodes;
this is a component bound, not a kinetic-energy bound.

Over the same 1/60 s physical interval, the new measurements are:

| Outer dt | Steps | Advection energy loss | Projection energy loss | Total energy loss | Final relative field L2 error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1/60 s | 1 | 0.7369% | 7.2396% | 7.9765% | 4.2388% |
| 1/120 s | 2 | 1.1734% | 3.6091% | 4.7825% | 2.5264% |
| 1/240 s | 4 | 2.5784% | 1.7890% | 4.3674% | 2.3821% |

Compared with the linear measurements above, bounded cubic sampling reduces
the accumulated advection loss at all three step sizes and lowers total loss.
The pressure-projection contribution remains essentially unchanged and is
still the largest single-step loss at 1/60 s. Accumulated interpolation loss
still grows as the interval is split into more samples, although the reduction
is material. The near-zero-step cubic control retains 99.9999753% of initial
energy with 1.63e−7 relative field L2 error.

All four runs use unchanged source fingerprints and report no WebGPU validation
errors. These probes measure the full production stage sequence but do not set
a new finite-step fidelity threshold. Receipts:
`artifacts/analytic-motion/vortex-cubic-dt60.json`,
`vortex-cubic-dt120.json`, `vortex-cubic-dt240.json`, and
`vortex-cubic-zero-dt.json`.
