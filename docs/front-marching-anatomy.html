§5 — what the paper prescribes

Two marches, one operation

Verbatim, §5
…we store level set values φ on cell centers of the octree, and solve for fast marching in uniform regions similar to Foster and Fedkiw [2001]. Near T-junctions we adopt the approach of Sethian and Vladimirsky [2000] and run fast marching on a tetrahedral mesh.redistancing
To extrapolate velocities outside the liquid region we first interpolate velocities from power faces to regular octree faces and use an approach similar to fast marching, this time operating on faces instead of cells and copying over the velocity value from the face closest to the free surface.velocity extrapolation
…maintenance of the narrow band is efficient enough to not be the bottleneck, even though we rely on a serial Fast Marching method.the paper's own cost note

The load-bearing sentence

“Copying over the velocity value from the face closest to the free surface” defines the extrapolated field as a closest-point transform over the seed set — not as the limit of a propagation. That is a definition of the answer, not of a method, so any procedure that finds the nearest seed is a legal implementation. Everything below trades on that: our marches are unordered parallel transforms, and they are exact rather than approximate because the target was never an ordering.

March 1 — our implementation

φ redistance: a jump-flood closest-point transform

The serial FMM and its runtime selector are deleted. Production redistance is a fail-closed JFA-CPT over the sparse fine narrow band: instead of propagating a distance, every band cell carries the index of its nearest interface crossing and distance is recomputed exactly from that closest point at the end. One workgroup per resident support page, over an unordered compacted page list.

The stride-1 and stride-2 floods run at 41–53 % occupancy and 68–73 % ALU, pulling 5–9 GB/s. One voxel per lane, dense inner loop, no divergent addressing. Hold that number — the velocity march reaches 34 % ALU at best.

Why a ladder, not a sweep

Reach is the sum of the descending strides, not the first one: a schedule starting at P covers P+P/2+…+1 = 2P−1, and the binary decomposition of any offset below that sum is exactly what the passes walk. Band 4 therefore needs a first stride of 4, not 8.

An ordered 6-direction sweep is not a substitute. Dispatch is one workgroup per support page, so a sweep propagates 4 cells plus halo — the same pass count, minus the 32-lane subgroup reduction, plus divergent workgroup-memory addressing. Measured 26 % worse.

The trailing 1s

Sparse pages break the clean binary landing: a closest-point route can turn across a page corner and need several local hops. Two repairs covered the mini dam and left 176,898 ocean band samples unresolved at the first rolling-wave expansion, so cold runs five. The fallback ladder is encoded every frame and dispatches zero workgroups when unused — that is most of the 0.046 ms bucketed row above, 25 encoded passes costing 2 µs each.

March 2 — our implementation

The Section 5 chain, and the wall inside it

The velocity march is not one pass. It is a ten-stage publication that builds the face graph, decides which faces demand support, seeds the ones touching liquid, marches, and interpolates back onto power faces. The whole chain runs twice per advance — once when the topology epoch is committed, once after the fine surface settles. Click a stage.

The expensive part

17.88 ms and 17.73 ms, at 34 % and 28 % ALU, moving 0.64 and 1.59 GB/s. Nothing about that says bandwidth and nothing says arithmetic. The kernel is shape-bound: a whole 128-lane workgroup is spent on one closest-seed query, and for most of that query 127 of its lanes have nothing to do.

Note which counter is which. Occupancy still reads 21 % and 30 % because the idle lanes are resident — parked on a workgroup barrier, holding their slots, retiring nothing. The machine is not starved of threads. It is holding threads that have no work.

Landing in the measured band needs roughly 1–2 node steps per leaf block visited. That is a weakly-pruned search: the query is opening many blocks, not descending decisively to one.

Corridor components

Before the march, 17 rounds of pointer-jumping union–find label every face row with its connected component of the demand corridor. A query then rejects any BVH subtree whose component differs — real pruning, and a correctness fix.

The corridor is not connected in general. On the large dam, one step produced an island of 1316 of 6209 support rows, all size 1 and 2, along the floor, with no seed inside it and no catalog incidence leaving it. When a component carries no seed on an axis, globalSearch drops the component filter for those queries rather than leaving them unresolved.

The candidate scan was never the cost

The predecessor design relaxed a changed frontier, and its inner loop gathered up to 30 incident rows × 4 quadrants = 120 candidates per destination. That looked like the wall. Running the whole scan twice — result-preserving, zero tripwires — moved the pass from 39.68 to 43.52 ms. One complete scan therefore costs ~3.84 ms of 39.68, against a 3.65 ms A/A floor. The other ~36 ms was the frontier machinery around it.

Do not ablate a fixed point

Collapsing that scan to a single candidate made the pass 2× slower (82.59 ms) and moved a neighbouring pass by +8.6 ms — because picking a non-closest face changes the marched field, so the iteration stops converging. An ablation that perturbs a fixed-point iteration measures its own divergence, not the loop you removed.

Where that leaves it

What is still on the table

Structural, not micro

Both the old relaxation and the new packet BVH spend their time on query shape, not on the arithmetic inside a query. The lever is the ratio of node steps to leaf blocks, and the number of lanes idle during a node step — which is a mapping decision, not an instruction-selection one.

The obvious unspent move is to stop giving one query a whole workgroup: batch many queries per workgroup and let lanes traverse independently, or make the stack step itself parallel over a wider node fan-out so descending the tree does not cost 127 idle lanes.

And the free one

The chain runs twice per advance — topology-commit and settled-fine — for 35.61 ms of march. The second site exists because the fine surface settles after the epoch is committed. Whether both publications are load-bearing on a step where topology did not actually change is a question the counters can answer, and it is worth 17.9 ms if the answer is no.
