A pass-by-pass account of what the sparse-voxel renderer does to produce the frame above: what each of the thirteen GPU passes computes, which structure it reads, and what the hardware actually does while it runs. The default presentation path — rasterized brick proxies for primary visibility, cone-traced visibility for lighting, deferred relighting — and, at length, the analytic signed-distance solids that are the set and are also the cost. The thesis

This frame is not bound by voxel traversal, by cone tracing, or by pixels. It is bound by one pass that shades every object standing in front of every other object. Sparse voxel exact live-scene primitive visibility — the pass that resolves the scene's analytic signed-distance solids — takes of a frame. Everything else the renderer does, including the whole sparse-voxel octree primary and the entire lighting pipeline, adds up to .

The cause is a two-line property of its fragment shader. It returns @builtin(frag_depth) and it calls discard. Either one alone tells a tile-based GPU that a fragment's depth and coverage are unknown until the fragment has been shaded, so hidden-surface removal cannot run and every proxy box covering a pixel evaluates its own signed-distance field. A covered pixel here sits under a median of such proxies and a 99th percentile of , and of the scene's primitives resolve their hit by a bounded sphere trace rather than a closed-form root.

Dropping both — a timing probe whose image is wrong, but which shows what the hardware is willing to reject — takes the frame from to , a paired . That is an over-statement, and §5.3 measures by how much: a sound front-to-back resolve still has to march proxies per covered pixel rather than , so the ordering itself is worth . The pass immediately before this one in the same command buffer already contains that design, and costs .

And the cost is not spread evenly over the set. The bonsai is of the records — — but it accounts for of all covering-proxy samples and, once each record's field is priced by what its shader loop actually folds over, for of the frame's distance-function evaluations. Section 5 is about that.

Counter coverage
GPU hardware counters are device-wide and cannot be filtered by process. Only samples taken while no other process had GPU work in flight are used. Per-pass time is exact regardless — it filters by process. Occupancy, ALU and bandwidth are directional.

Four caveats that matter when reading everything below.

  1. Label isolation is free here. The traced run splits the frame's WebGPU passes into individually-labelled Metal encoders so each carries its own GPU timestamp. It measured /frame against a clean, untraced, un-isolated run's over three interleaved samples. Per-pass attribution therefore describes the frame that ships, not a distorted one.
  2. Vertex, Fragment and Compute are separate hardware channels and they overlap. Apple's tiler runs one pass's fragment work against the next pass's binning. Every row of the pass table is one channel of one encoder; summing all rows slightly exceeds the frame wall, and the union — device busy — does not.
  3. The first four frames are excluded. They carry Metal's first-use residency work: the glass pass's vertex stage alone reports 225–308 ms on frames 1–4 and 20–50 µs thereafter. steady-state frames are analysed out of complete ones.
  4. The machine was shared. held GPU work inside the capture window, which is why counter coverage is low and why the frame wall spreads . Every timing conclusion below is interleaved and paired for exactly that reason.
§2

The scene, as the renderer sees it

hero-garden-hose is a dry scene: systems.fluid is false, no solver is constructed, and WebGPULiveSvoScene stands in for one — it owns an OctreeSparseBrickWorld and implements the narrow source interface the renderer attaches to, with no transport, projection or level-set authority at all (lib/webgpu-live-svo-scene.ts:61). Every millisecond below is therefore render cost, with nothing simulated behind it.

The set is a raised porcelain pond. Its vessel — coping crest, basin, and the ground outside it — is one generated heightfield rather than geometry, so the water would meet the rim exactly where the rim is. Everything standing on that heightfield is an analytic signed-distance solid, and there is no triangle mesh anywhere in the frame.

Domain and lattice
Published to the GPU

Two entries in the right-hand column are worth pausing on, because they are what the lighting passes in §4 actually read.

The node-mip opacity pyramid is a sparse, page-backed mip chain over the octree: 8³ interiors with a one-voxel apron, four channels (solid mean, solid max, fluid mean, fluid max), 4 000 B per page, pages resident here. It is what a cone samples instead of a ray marching leaves. Publication is all-or-nothing by design — a truncated pyramid leaks light rather than coarsening, so OctreeSparseBrickWorld withdraws the whole thing if any base page is omitted, and cone lighting silently falls back to exact traversal for every shadow and GI ray. That failure is a 15× render regression that reads as a renderer bug; on this scene it published cleanly.

The tetrahedral radiance atlas is four directional lobes of outgoing radiance over the same pages, — the cached bounce the GI cones gather rather than re-trace.

§5

The signed-distance set, in detail

The set is not geometry. Every object in the frame except the terrain is one 64-byte record naming an analytic solid, and the renderer resolves each one exactly, per pixel, in a fragment shader. That is what makes the scene cheap to author, cheap to store — records is — and free of any level-of-detail decision: a floret is silhouetted exactly at any distance, and the same canopy rendered at 25 mm, 50 mm and 100 mm voxels produces three byte-identical frames, because a scenery primitive's primary visibility never goes through the voxel payload at all.

It is also, at this scale, of the frame. This section is about where inside the set that cost actually sits, which is not where the pixels are.

5.1  What one marched hit costs

A kind is declared once, in lib/svo-primitive-kinds.ts: u32 tag, flags, normal policy, what the three dimension floats mean, the conservative local extent and the rotation-invariant bounding radius. Every consumer keys an exhaustive Record<SvoPrimitiveKindName, T> off it, so a kind cannot be added to one path and be silently missing from another — which is the failure that table exists to prevent, because an unknown kind used to fall through to an ellipsoid in the node-mip oracle, to 1e20 in the voxelizer, and to a vertex-shader bail in this very raster.

The flag that decides cost is marchedIntersection. A kind carrying it has no closed-form root: its hit is a bounded sphere trace of its own distance function, capped at SVO_PRIMITIVE_MARCH_ITERATIONS = , over the interval the proxy box brackets. of primitives here are marched.

And a march step is not one distance call. For a smooth-union-cluster — the aggregate kind, of them in this scene — a step evaluates a whole procedural field, and the field is a fold over lobes, segments or lattice cells. Then the shading normal costs four more whole field evaluations: a tetrahedral gradient rather than the obvious six-tap central difference, precisely because "an evaluation is roughly sixty sphere distances and the march converges in about eight steps, so the two extra taps would be a fifth of the whole trace" (lib/svo-primitive-abi.ts:2600).

The 56 outer cells are rejected by a distance to their unjittered centre, which needs no hash, so the realised width is between 8 and 64 per octave.

Coverage is exact: a ray/AABB slab test of the hero camera's own pixel rays against each primitive's conservative world box, which is the box the vertex stage emits. Evaluations are that same exact coverage multiplied by each record's fold width and by the shader's typical eight-step march — derived, and stated as a share rather than an absolute because the step count of a missing march is not modelled here. The map's shape does not depend on that constant; only its scale does.

The map above is a count. This is the thing the count is counting. 5.4  Inside one march

And this is what one of those bars costs. A marched kind has no closed-form root: the fragment sphere-traces its own distance function inside the proxy's bracket, and for a cluster each step of that trace evaluates a whole procedural field — a fold over lobes, segments or lattice cells. Then the shading normal costs four more whole evaluations.

5.5  The bonsai

records — of the set — carrying of the coverage and of the evaluations. It is worth taking apart, because every decision in it is defensible and the aggregate is still the most expensive object in the frame by a wide margin.

Why it is built out of clusters

The crown is one wide plate whose entire character is the floret structure covering it. Spelled explicitly, that is roughly 2·area / (π·w²) records for a floret of width w — so halving the floret quadruples the bill — against a scene-wide budget of 4 096 primitive-candidate leaves that is a cliff rather than a slope: above it the candidate build is simply not called and the whole set stops being drawn. The reference's six-millimetre floret was unreachable with the entire scene budget.

A cluster evaluates its packing in the shader, so its grain costs no records at all and that economy stops applying. What it cannot do is be both granular and closed — a lattice only stops letting rays through at r ≥ period·√3/2, and at that ratio the packing is solid, the envelope's hard max cuts it off at exactly the envelope, and the lobe draws as a bare ellipsoid. So the crown is two records per lobe: a lattice cluster at a ratio where florets are distinct, and an ellipsoid core one granulation-depth under it that no ray gets past.

The trunk and limbs were a chain of cones with a sphere at every node. That construction is exact and it still rendered wrong: a hard union is C⁰ but not C¹, so the shading normal steps at every junction and a smooth taper came out as a stack of faceted pipes. Subdividing does not help, because subdividing adds junctions. A tapered sweep fuses its segments with a smooth minimum — C¹ through the junction — and costs one record for a whole run instead of two per segment.

        Apple's tile-based deferred renderer removes hidden surfaces before shading, but only when it
        can know a fragment's depth and coverage from the rasterizer alone. A shader that returns
        @builtin(frag_depth) moves the depth decision after shading; a shader

        writes no depth and shades nothing: it appends the instance index into a fixed 24-entry

        append. A second full-screen pass then walks each pixel's candidates front-to-back exactly
        once. Overflow beyond 24 candidates re-runs the historical direct fragment in an isolated
        pipeline, so capacity is a performance parameter and never changes the image.

How it works

        

          A cone is marched, not a ray. Step width follows the continuous cone diameter and each
          sample is a trilinear fetch from the node-mip opacity pyramid. Coverage is
          C0-continuous in lod: a single
          floor(lod) fetch made accumulated opacity jump wherever the level
          incremented, which rendered as concentric isodistance rings around point lights, so the two
          bracketing levels are blended — but only inside a trailing transition band, because
          full-range blending doubled per-step fetches and cost +35 % frame time.
        

        

          The pass runs at half rate () over an 8 × 8 workgroup grid.
          Each lane reads the 2 × 2 full-resolution G-buffer footprint under it and tests it for
          homogeneity — same identity, depth within 1 %, normals within 1e-4 of parallel. Homogeneous
          lanes resolve visibility for the whole quad; the rest enqueue their coordinate into an
          atomic boundary queue that a third dispatch in the same encoder retraces exactly.
          Reconstruction back to full rate is full-res-relight: the reduced
          plane carries only visibility, and material and BRDF are re-evaluated at full resolution in
          the deferred pass.
